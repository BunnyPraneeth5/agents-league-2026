## Relationship Between Meeting Hours and Study Completion

The synthetic workload data shows a clear inverse pattern: employees with fewer meeting hours tend to have more focus hours available for learning. L-1004 has the lightest meeting load at 9 hours per week and the highest focus capacity at 24 hours per week. L-1001 also has a favorable pattern, with 11 meeting hours and 21 focus hours.

Employees with heavier meeting loads have less protected focus time. L-1005 has 22 meeting hours and only 10 focus hours, while L-1003 has 19 meeting hours and 12 focus hours. These profiles are more likely to experience slower study completion, fragmented learning sessions, and lower retention after complex topics.

## Optimal Focus Hours for Learning

The strongest learning conditions appear when employees have at least 18 focus hours per week. L-1001 and L-1004 both meet this threshold and are good candidates for deeper certification work, including lab-heavy study blocks.

Employees with 10 to 14 focus hours per week, such as L-1002, L-1003, and L-1005, should use shorter and more frequent study sessions. They may need longer calendar timelines to complete certifications with 50 or more recommended study hours.

## Scheduling Recommendations

- For L-1001 and L-1004, schedule two or three 90-minute study blocks per week, preferably in the morning.
- For L-1002, use evening study sessions with lighter reading or review work after high-meeting days.
- For L-1003, protect afternoon learning time and avoid placing study blocks directly after long meeting clusters.
- For L-1005, reduce meeting load where possible before assigning advanced certification milestones.
- For all employees, schedule practice tests during high-focus windows rather than at the end of meeting-heavy days.

## Operating Guidance for Managers

Managers should treat focus hours as a leading indicator for certification progress. When focus hours fall below 12 per week, study plans should be adjusted to smaller weekly goals and extended completion timelines. For advanced certifications, managers should aim to create at least two protected focus blocks per week before expecting exam readiness.
